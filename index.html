About

Background

I'm a final-year Information Technology student developing toward research in artificial intelligence and natural language processing. My academic background is in IT rather than linguistics or computer science by name, so most of what I know about NLP research — dataset construction, annotation, model evaluation — I've learned by building it directly, mainly through my final-year project.

I'm interested in how language behaves computationally: how models handle text that mixes languages and writing systems, how much data a language actually needs before NLP methods work on it, and how those methods can be applied to education. My current work is on Pashto — a language I speak, and one where almost no labeled data existed before I started building it.

I'm early in this path. I don't yet have publications, and I try to describe my work at the stage it's actually at rather than further along. What I do have is a consistent habit of building — a fine-tuned model, an annotation platform, a dataset, an applied learning tool — and documenting the process, including the parts that didn't work the first time. I'm hoping to continue this work at the graduate level.

Current Research

Sentiment analysis of Romanized Pashto text

My current research applies the themes above to a specific, concrete case: Pashto as it's actually typed online — mixed with Urdu and English, written in Roman script, with no standard spelling. There was effectively no labeled sentiment data for this variety of the language when I started, so the first part of the work has been building the dataset itself, then fine-tuning and evaluating a model on it.

01

Romanized Pashto Sentiment Analysis

Research · Ongoing (FYP)
Problem
Almost no labeled sentiment data exists for Romanized, code-mixed Pashto — a spelling-inconsistent variety with no orthographic standard.
Approach
Built a ~4,000-sentence manually annotated dataset (Positive/Negative/Neutral) from Facebook, Twitter, and WhatsApp, then fine-tuned a multilingual transformer for classification.
Technologies
XLM-RoBERTa, Google Colab (T4 GPU), FastAPI serving platform.
What was learned
An early 91% weighted F1 turned out to be inflated by a data-leakage bug in the train/validation split; correcting it produced a lower but trustworthy 87.8%. Reliable evaluation matters more than a high headline number.

Note 001 — Fine-tuning XLM-RoBERTa for Romanized Pashto Sentiment

FYP
Question

Can a multilingual transformer, fine-tuned on a small manually-annotated dataset, reach usable accuracy on 3-class sentiment for Romanized Pashto?

Dataset

~4,000 manually annotated Romanized Pashto sentences (Positive / Negative / Neutral), sourced from Facebook, Twitter, and WhatsApp.

Method

Fine-tuned XLM-RoBERTa on Google Colab (T4 GPU), on the reasoning that its multilingual pretraining should transfer reasonably well given vocabulary overlap with Urdu, Hindi, and English tokens, even though Pashto itself was unseen during pretraining.

Experiment

Standard train/validation split, weighted F1 as the primary evaluation metric.

Result
First run
91.0%
After fix
87.8%
Interpretation

The initial 91% weighted F1 was inflated by a data-leakage bug in the train/validation split. Once diagnosed and corrected, performance settled at 87.8% weighted F1 — lower, but trustworthy.

Limitation

Dataset size (~4,000 sentences) is small relative to high-resource sentiment benchmarks. Class balance and dialectal/spelling variation in Romanized Pashto remain open questions.

Next step

Expand and quality-control the dataset using the annotation platform, then prepare a manuscript describing the dataset and results (see Publications).
